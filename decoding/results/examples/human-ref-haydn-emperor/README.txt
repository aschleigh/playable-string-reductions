Demonstration only (not an experiment): a real human piano arrangement against our piano arranger.

Human reference: Muzio Clementi, "God Save the Emperor. Composed by Dr. Haydn", Introduction to the Art of Playing on
the Piano Forte, Op. 42 (London, 1803), Lesson XXVII. Digital edition: Mutopia Project (ed. Javier Ruiz-Alma, 2014),
CC BY-SA 4.0, https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/ClementiM/O42/clementi-op42/ (MIDI clementi-op42-les27-haydn-adag.mid).
Source: Haydn, String Quartet Op. 76 No. 3, II, bars 1-20 (the hymn theme), OpenScore String Quartets.

Alignment: Clementi plays a a (b c)(b c); the quartet theme is a a b c c'. Clementi's quarters 0-64 map to the
quartet's 0-64 and his last c (80-96) to the quartet's c' (64-80); his second b is left out. Melody pitch match with
the quartet's first violin: 34/34, 35/38, 17/19.
Caveats: Op. 76 No. 3 is in our training split (the model saw the quartet, never any human arrangement). Clementi
set the hymn, not necessarily Haydn's quartet voicing. Both our models are decoded masked, melody-first, under checker-v7.
